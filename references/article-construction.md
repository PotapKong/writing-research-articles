## Default article structure

Unless the user requests another format:

### 1. Working title
Create a strong, publication-quality headline. Must carry a real argument or tension — not a bland topic label.

### 2. Optional subhead
Use when it improves framing. If the headline already carries enough context and force, go straight into the lede.

### 3. Lede
Open with a sharp, informative paragraph. Do not open with generic scene-setting. One strong setup, one framing claim, one reason the reader should care now.

### 4. Body

**Standard section options:**
- What changed
- Why it matters
- What the numbers show
- Who benefits and who is exposed
- What the market still misreads
- Risks, constraints, and counterarguments
- What to watch next

**Default pattern for long analytical pieces:**
1. Opening hook and framing thesis
2. Historical or structural context
3. Data section with key numbers
4. Company, country, or platform breakdowns (if relevant)
5. Risks and counterarguments
6. Conclusion and investor or operator takeaway

### 5. Closing
End with a line that resolves the argument cleanly. Avoid motivational or theatrical endings.

### 6. Optional disclaimer
Required when the article touches markets, investing, tokens, macro positioning, or public securities — unless the user explicitly says not to include it.

## Body architecture rules

- organize by logic, not filler
- each section must advance the core argument
- if one block is weakly supported: remove it, reduce to one cautious sentence, or move to a brief mention
- a tighter article beats a broader but weaker one
- match structure to domain; do not force every topic into a market/investment frame
- for AI and technical topics, separate what the system can do, how it works, what evidence supports it, where it fails, and what it changes for operators or users

## Domain-specific structure options

### AI and agentic systems

- What changed in the agent stack
- What the source actually shows
- The architecture or workflow underneath
- Why it matters for builders, operators, or businesses
- Where the claims are overextended
- What to test before adopting it
- What to watch next

### Source discovery articles

When the user first asks for links or topics, do not write the article yet.
Return the Source Shortlist, then wait for the user's choice. After selection,
turn the chosen source into a new article using the wider Fact Grid.

## Modular drafting

Draft each section so it can be cut or rewritten without breaking the whole article. After any section is cut, rebalance transitions and conclusion so the article reads as intentional rather than patched.
